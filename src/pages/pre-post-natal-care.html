Pre and Post Natal Care

Ayurveda's guidance on diet, routine and care for the mother, before and after delivery, and for the baby.

Pre Natal Care

"Evam kurvati hi arogya-bala-varna-samvahana-sampadam upetam jnatinam shreshtam apatyam janayati"

If a pregnant woman is cared for as advised, she will give birth to a healthy child, physically strong, radiant and well nourished.

Added to an already busy life at home and at work comes the most beautiful time in a woman's life: giving life. With today's nuclear families, there is often no one to guide the mother through pregnancy and early motherhood. Ayurveda prescribes specific nutritional measures to protect the woman and keep her healthy.

In one of its best-known teaching analogies, Ayurveda compares conception to the germination of a seed and its growth into a sapling. When the male and female seeds unite, an embryo (garbha) is created. Ayurveda gives great importance to the quality of the seed (ovum), and to the mother's uterus as the ideal terrain in which it grows.

Like its approach to longevity, Ayurveda's approach to pregnancy and childbirth is holistic. Its recommendations cover the diet, behaviour, activities and even the spiritual practices of the mother, and of the father as well.

Correct behavioural patterns

Simple exercises, a proper diet and a healthy lifestyle help give birth to a strong baby and help both mother and baby in the years to come.

A balanced diet and the right herbal drinks at different stages of pregnancy give strength to the growing foetus. In the last stage of pregnancy, certain procedures help prepare the birth passage naturally, supporting an easier delivery and making labour more bearable.

A couple hoping for a healthy child should both take care of their diet, activities, behaviour and emotional state before and after conception, and throughout the pregnancy.

General rules from conception to delivery

The mother-to-be should:

  • Stay cheerful
  • Be clean, neat and well dressed
  • Wear simple, comfortable clothes
  • Sleep in a clean environment free of mosquitoes and other insects

Her food should be tasty, largely liquid, moist and nourishing, include all six rasas (tastes), and be prepared with deepana herbs that improve appetite and digestion.

The mother-to-be should avoid:

  • Excessive sexual activity, particularly in early and late pregnancy
  • Overeating or fasting
  • Sleeping in the daytime and staying up late at night
  • Tight clothes and tight belts
  • Seeing or hearing things that cause sorrow, anger, fear or distress
  • Travelling on rough roads
  • Squatting for long, or sitting in an uncomfortable position or on a hard surface
  • Lifting heavy things or bending for long periods
  • Oil massage and similar treatments unless specifically advised
  • Suppressing natural urges
  • Dry, stale, fermented, heavy, hot or strong food, alcohol and meat (fish is allowed)

Ayurveda also describes nine complaints that arise because of pregnancy, called garbhopadravas: nausea, loss of appetite, vomiting, dry mouth, fever, swelling (oedema), anaemia, diarrhoea and retention of urine. Their treatment is described in detail.

As far as possible, medication is avoided in the first three months of pregnancy. Thousands of years ago Ayurveda stressed safe motherhood: healthy formation and development of the foetus, a comfortable full-term delivery and the continued health of the mother. Post Natal and Baby Care

This phase is called the Sutika period, from prasuta, the mother after delivery. Opinions differ on its length, from six weeks to six months; some consider it to last until menstruation resumes.

A special diet is recommended for the new mother to make up for the loss of fluids, tissues (dhatus) and blood during pregnancy and delivery. She should eat light yet nourishing food during this period. Following this regimen helps her recover faster and regain and keep her health.

Aims of post natal care

  • Relief from weakness and body pain
  • Supporting the uterus to regain its shape and size
  • Improving breast milk production
  • Correcting metabolism and digestion
  • Repairing and toning the muscles
  • Restoring Agni (digestive fire)
  • Calming and normalising Vata

Diet for the lactating mother

  • Ghee and oils in adequate amounts improve digestion, ease bowel movements, nourish the tissues and calm the Vata that is aggravated during childbirth.
  • Avoid canned foods and hot, pungent and spicy food, which reduce milk secretion. A simple, bland vegetarian diet is best for a nursing mother.
  • Avoid pollution and exposure to infection, since an infection in the mother during lactation can pass to the child.
  • Take care with medicines during lactation, since many pass into breast milk and can affect the baby.
  • Even with plenty of milk, semi-solid food can be started around four months. Breastfeeding is gradually reduced and solid food given from about one year.
  • A traditional first food for the child is a semi-solid paste of well-cooked rice with sugar and cardamom.
