Wound Management

Wound management in Ayurveda (Vrana chikitsa) for diabetic foot ulcers, varicose ulcers and wounds that will not heal.

What is a wound?

Any damage that breaks the continuity of the skin is called a wound.

Types: non-healing ulcers are classified pathologically into three:

  • Non-specific ulcers
  • Specific ulcers
  • Malignant ulcers

A non-healing or chronic wound is one that does not improve after four weeks or does not heal in eight weeks. These include diabetic foot ulcers, venous ulcers and non-healing surgical wounds. Examples:

  • Traumatic ulcer
  • Varicose ulcer
  • Trophic ulcer
  • Ulcers in diabetes and most arterial diseases

The wound healing process

Healing of skin wounds is a classic example of regeneration and repair combined. A clean wound, unless affected by adverse factors, starts healing a few hours after injury, and normally needs no specific treatment to heal in its natural sequence. But this natural process is often prevented by various retarding factors, so it is important to understand the healing pattern and what interferes with it. Wound healing is a series of complex, interdependent and overlapping stages.

Vrana (wound) chikitsa in Ayurveda

A wound is called vrana because it covers the site, and its scar remains for life even after healing.

Important clinical classification:

  • Shuddha vrana: clean wound
  • Dushta vrana: unhealthy, infected wound
  • Ruhyamana vrana: healing wound
  • Rudha vrana: healed wound

According to Charaka: Vata, Pitta and Kapha, vitiated by their causes, settle in the external pathway (Bahyamarga) and produce innate (Nija) vranas, for example Madhumehaja vrana (diabetic wound).

Role of Pitta dosha in the development of Dushta vrana: when vitiated Pitta becomes aggravated over time, it overpowers Vata and Kapha and sets up suppuration through the blood. Vitiated Pitta "burns" the Rakta and results in the formation of pus (Chakrapani and Dalhana).

Management

Sushruta described sixty measures (Shashti upakrama) for managing a wound, from its first appearance to the rehabilitation of the scar, giving foremost importance to Shodhana and Ropana in managing Dushta vrana.

Shodhana (cleaning)

Shodhana comprises two kinds of purification:

  • Antah shodhana: internal purification
  • Bahya shodhana: external purification

Internal purification is done by Vamana, Virechana and Vasti (Panchakarma). External purification is done in two ways: Raktamokshana, including leech therapy, and application of medicines.

Ropana (healing)

Ropana means whatever promotes or speeds up healing. Apart from anti-infective and debriding agents, modern medicine has few measures that actively promote healing. Sushruta gave special attention to Ropana, describing it in the form of kashaya (decoctions), varti (wicks), ghrita (medicated ghee), taila (medicated oil), choorna (powders) and more.

If pus is not properly cleaned from a wound, what happens?

As fire fanned by the wind sweeps through a field and burns it rapidly, pus that is not drained from a wound eats away the muscle, vessels and nerves.
