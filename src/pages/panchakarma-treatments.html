Panchakarma Treatments

The five treatment modalities of Ayurveda for removing aggravated doshas (Vata, Pitta and Kapha), used to maintain health in the healthy and to treat disease in the sick.

The five treatments are Vamana karma, Virechana karma, Nasya karma, Vasti karma and Raktamokshana. Each course is planned by the physician after a consultation and follows the seasons; see the season guide on our home page.

VamanaTherapeutic emesis

Vamana is a therapeutic procedure in which excess or vitiated Kapha dosha is removed from the body by inducing vomiting. In healthy people Vamana is done in Vasanta ritu (March–April) to remove Kapha accumulated through seasonal changes. In patients, it is done when there is excessive vitiated Kapha and related disease.

VirechanaTherapeutic purgation

Virechana removes excess or vitiated Pitta dosha from the body through controlled purgation. In healthy people it is done in Sharad ritu (September–October) to remove Pitta accumulated in the body and prevent Pitta disorders. It clears toxins, especially Pitta, accumulated in the digestive tract, liver and gallbladder, and thoroughly cleanses the gastrointestinal tract. Done under supervision, it is a safe procedure.

NasyaNasal medication

Nasya karma removes excess Kapha from the head and neck region using medicines given through the nose. "Nasa hi shiraso dwaram": the nose is the gateway to the head. Diseases of the region above the shoulders, including the brain, nose, ears, eyes, throat, mouth, neck and hair, are treated with Nasya. Healthy people should also have Nasya in Vasanta ritu (March–April) to clear excess Kapha from this region.

VastiMedicated enema

Vasti is considered the best of all treatments for its many effects. It is described as "Ardha chikitsa": its effect equals half of all the treatments described in Ayurveda. Vasti is the Panchakarma treatment for aggravated Vata and Vata-related diseases. It cleanses all the channels of the body and so purifies it.

  • Niruha vasti (Asthapana or Kashaya vasti): a decoction suited to the disease, mixed with salt, honey, ghee and other ingredients, given on an empty stomach. Especially indicated for diseases of the large intestine and digestive tract.
  • Yapana vasti: a type of Niruha vasti that nourishes all the tissues of the body, especially soft tissue, muscle and sperm. It slows ageing, sustains life and improves strength and lustre.
  • Vaitarana vasti: indicated in sciatica, lumbago, spondylitis, and pain and swelling of the knees, shoulders and lower back. Also given in chronic fever and infertility.
  • Kshira vasti: especially indicated for osteoporosis of the knees and spine. It also improves strength and lustre.
  • Sangrahika vasti (Piccha vasti): for diseases of the gastrointestinal tract, especially bleeding disorders and irritable bowel syndrome (IBS).
  • Anuvasana vasti (Sneha or Matra vasti): medicated oil or ghee given in a small dose. It increases strength and vitality and nourishes all the body's tissues. It can be given even to children, the elderly and weak patients.
  • Uttara vasti: given through the urethral route in men and the vaginal route in women, for diseases of the urinary system and of the uterus and vagina. It is done only after purification by Asthapana vasti.
RaktamokshanaBloodletting

A small amount of blood is let out of the body under careful monitoring. This removes impure blood and improves circulation, and also helps heal chronic non-healing wounds. It is done in two main ways:

  • Shastra visravana: Prachana (blood let through multiple small incisions) and Siravyadha (venepuncture)
  • Anushastra visravana: Jalaukavacharana, the application of leeches (leech therapy)
