Anushastra Karma (para surgical procedures)

Shalya Tantra is one of the important branches of Ayurveda, describing surgical and para-surgical techniques for managing various diseases. Sushruta, the father of surgery, classified it systematically, and his clinical material and principles of management are valid even today. Anushastra Karma means procedures performed with non-surgical materials or instruments. Acharya Sushruta described several, including Kshara Karma, Kshara Sutra, Agnikarma, Siravyadha and Jalaukavacharana (leech therapy), for conditions such as fissure, fistula, piles, pilonidal sinus and non-healing wounds.

Agnikarma

In Agnikarma, heat (agni) is applied directly or indirectly with different materials to treat disease. Any procedure involving agni directly or indirectly is considered Agnikarma. Agnikarma has a sterilising effect: its heat destroys pathogens, so wounds after Agnikarma are rarely infected.

Heat is applied to the painful points using a shalaka, an instrument made of panchadhatu. Agnikarma is indicated in disorders of Asthi (bone), Sandhi (joint) and Snayu (ligament and tendon), and in Arsha (piles), Arbuda (tumour), Bhagandara (fistula-in-ano), ligament injury, tendonitis, joint pain and Gridhrasi (sciatica). It is effective in acute as well as chronic pain.

Fissure in Ano

A fissure is a longitudinal tear at the lower end of the anus, and one of the most painful conditions of the anal region. Sometimes a small tag of swollen skin forms at the edge of the anus, called a sentinel tag or sentinel pile, which remains even after the fissure heals unless removed. Fissures are common in young and middle-aged people, and because of poor food habits are also seen in children.

Symptoms

  • Severe burning, cutting pain during and after passing stool
  • Severe constipation
  • A drop or streak of fresh blood during defecation
  • Sentinel pile (a tag of skin at the outer edge of the anus)
  • Itching
  • Hard stool

Causes

  • Severe constipation or dysentery
  • Surgery (such as piles surgery)
  • Sphincter hypertonia (spasm of the anus)
  • Repeated childbirth
  • Excessive use of ointments
  • Excessive use of laxatives

Treating fissure

Self help: correcting constipation. Adopt a high-fibre diet, eat according to your appetite, avoid fast food, sip water while eating and exercise regularly.

Sitz bath. The best way to reduce pain is a sitz bath in plain warm water or warm medicated water.

Agnikarma. A para-surgical procedure, a form of thermal cauterisation. Sentinel tags are removed by thermal cauterisation, and sphincterotomy is also performed by Agnikarma to reduce spasm of the anal ring.

Fistula in Ano (Bhagandara)

History of fistula

This disease has been known for more than 4,000 years. Despite tremendous developments in modern surgery, treating fistula-in-ano remains a challenge.

What is a fistula?

An anal fistula is an abnormal tube-like passage between the inside of the anal canal (or rectum) and the skin around the anus. One opening (the internal opening) is inside the anus or rectum and the other (the external opening) is on the skin around the anus. A tube-like track connects them.

Symptoms of fistula

  • Persistent sticky discharge of pus or stool from the external opening near the anus
  • Recurrent swelling and abscess on the buttock
  • Itching and a moist feeling in the anal area
  • Pain in a lump on the buttock
  • Gas escaping from the external skin opening

Causes of fistula

  • A neglected fissure can become infected and turn into a fistula
  • Spicy and fast food habits, sitting jobs
  • A complication of abscess, tuberculosis, cancer, inflammatory bowel disease, ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease

Diagnosis

  • Fistula probing
  • Fistulogram
  • MRI

Treating fistula with Kshara Sutra

Fistula is a challenge for modern medicine: antibiotics and advanced surgery cannot always remove the track and the infection, and fistulas often recur. At AyurRemedy we have treated patients whose fistulas had returned after repeated surgeries.

Kshara Sutra is a well-accepted Ayurvedic technique for fistula-in-ano. Its advantages:

  1. Low recurrence

  5. Fewer complications; incontinence is rare with this procedure

Kshara Sutra procedure

A malleable probe is passed to locate the internal opening. Once found, the probe is brought out through the anal verge and the track is threaded loosely with Kshara Sutra. Jatyadi oil (10 ml) is placed in the rectum and sterile gauze covers the anus and the external opening.

Weekly change of the thread. The first Kshara Sutra stays for 7 days, then is replaced with a fresh one by the rail-road technique. This is repeated every week until the track has completely cut through and healed. Any unhealthy granulation tissue is scraped off at the weekly visit to promote better healing.

Pilonidal Sinus (Nadi Vrana)

What is a pilonidal sinus?

"Pilonidal" means nest of hairs. A pilonidal sinus is an irregular tube-like track containing a tuft of hair. The cyst or abscess, with infected tissue, pus and blood, lies under the skin over the tailbone, between the buttocks. It is also called "jeep bottom" or barber's disease, as it is common in jeep drivers.

Who gets pilonidal sinus?

It is common in India, rare in children and in people over 40, and four times more common in men. Risk factors include:

  • A job involving a lot of sitting
  • Being overweight
  • Persistent irritation or injury to the area
  • A hairy, deep natal cleft
  • A family history of the condition

Symptoms of pilonidal cyst

  • An external sinus opening at the base of the spine between the buttocks
  • Pus discharge from a lump on the lower back
  • Recurrent swelling and abscess on the lower back
  • Fever and pain

Treating pilonidal sinus with Kshara Sutra

There is no daily dressing, no hospital admission and no bed rest, and patients usually return to routine work within a few hours.

Haemorrhoids (Piles)

The Sushruta Samhita described haemorrhoids as Arsha more than 4,000 years ago. The conservative treatment and Kshara Karma he described are used at AyurRemedy.

What are piles?

Haemorrhoids, also called piles, are swollen and inflamed veins in the anus and lower rectum. They may result from straining during bowel movements or from increased pressure on these veins during pregnancy.

Symptoms of piles

  • Bleeding (a splash in the pan)
  • Usually no pain
  • A mass prolapsing through the anus
  • Mucus discharge
  • Itching

Causes of piles

  • Chronic constipation
  • Straining while passing stool
  • Heredity
  • Straining during childbirth
  • Repeated diarrhoea or dysentery
  • Long hours of sitting or standing at work
  • Repeatedly lifting heavy weights
  • Habitually suppressing the urge to pass stool

Types of piles

By site:

  • External: cause severe pain when thrombosed
  • Internal: may cause painless bleeding
  • Interno-external

By degree:

  • First degree: no mass protrudes from the anus (bleeding only)
  • Second degree: the mass protrudes but returns on its own after passing stool
  • Third degree: the mass protrudes and has to be pushed back with a finger
  • Fourth degree: the mass stays permanently outside the anus

Preventing haemorrhoids

  • Eat high-fibre food
  • Drink plenty of fluids
  • Exercise daily
  • Don't strain while passing stool
  • Don't sit or stand in one place for long
  • Don't lift heavy weights continuously
  • Keep the anal area clean
